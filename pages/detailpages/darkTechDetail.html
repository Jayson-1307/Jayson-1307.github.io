Echoes From the shadows

Wat is Echoes From the Shadows

Echoes From the Shadows is a project I worked on during my studio semester (elective semester) in the third year of my HBO-ICT program. The studio was called Dark Tech, where the goal was to explore the “dark” side of IT, push boundaries, and investigate what ethical responses or counter-solutions could look like. I worked on Echoes From the Shadows together with four other students, and my technical role within the project was that of a Creative Technologist. In this context, a Creative Technologist uses technology to investigate problems and design and realise innovative, user-focused solutions.

The main goal of this project was to create awareness around spyware and surveillance. We chose to do this by designing both a Dark and a Light version of a surveillance interface. The Dark version represents an unethical, manipulative approach to surveillance, while the Light version focuses on transparency and responsible use. To support this concept, we purchased and programmed several microphones and cameras ourselves. The purpose of creating both interfaces was to clearly highlight the contrast between how easily this technology can be abused and how it could be implemented in an ethical and user-conscious way. To properly support these design choices, we conducted extensive research — both online and with real users. This included desk research, user tests, interviews and the analysis of user behaviour in scenarios we designed where participants were placed under surveillance.

The rest of this page showcases my contributions to the project and gives a broader view of how the project developed as a whole.

My Contribution

UX/UI Design

Designed both Dark and Light versions of the surveillance interface, focusing on ethical vs. unethical user interactions. Each iteration was tested and improved based on the user feedback. (low to medium to product)

Front-end Development

Implemented the interface designs in code, integrating visual and interactive elements.

Presentation Strategy at Society 5.0

Analysed why our first presentation day at a Tech-convention failed, identified the missing elements (lack of visibility, unclear value, low engagement), and developed a new presentation strategy. Implemented this improved setup on day two, resulting in significantly higher engagement and more valuable user insights.

This specifically taught me that a prototype doesn't always speak for itself, and that it's very important to present your work in the most intriguing way possible.

Hardware Integration

Programmed and helped setting up the microphones for the prototype

User Testing & Research

Conducted user tests, interviews, and surveys to gather insights on user behaviour and awareness of surveillance.

Ethical Considerations

Applied knowledge of dark patterns and ethical design principles to contrast responsible and irresponsible use of surveillance technology.

The Final Product

The final product of our project consisted of two distinct surveillance interfaces: a Dark version and a Light version. Both versions were designed to showcase contrasting approaches to surveillance technology, highlighting the ethical implications of each design choice.
